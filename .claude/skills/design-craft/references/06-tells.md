# The tells: how machine-made design gives itself away

Read before choosing a direction and again before review. None of these is wrong in itself; **landing in one by
default** is the failure. A brand that owns an ingredient (a cream ground, a serif, a warm accent) must earn it with
composition and content no template has.

## The default looks (generated work clusters here whatever the subject)

1. **Warm cream ground + high-contrast serif display + terracotta or red accent**, often with italic headlines,
   hairline rules and small tracked labels ("editorial").
2. **Near-black + one neon accent + glowing edges**, glass panels, gradient meshes, cyan on dark ("tech").
3. **Broadsheet pastiche**: hairlines everywhere, italic display serif, mono labels, dense columns with nothing in them.
4. **Flat saturated ink** with black boxes, hard offset shadows and heavy condensed display ("neobrutalist").
5. **Product-site skeleton**: white canvas, Inter, black or single-hue CTA, 6–8px buttons, 12px cards, a 96px section
   rhythm, "product UI fragments in cards", a dark featured pricing tier. Only the hue and radius change between sites.
6. **The template hero**: headline, subline, two pill buttons, then three icon cards, then logos, then testimonials.

## The tells, by family

- **Structure**: identical card grids; nested cards; icon tiles above headings; eyebrow chips; section numbers as
  ornament; a full sentence set at display size as the h1 with nothing else in the view; uniform spacing.
- **Surface**: gradient text; purple-blue gradients; glows and halos; glass; side stripes; ghost cards (hairline +
  wide shadow); decorative stripes, grids and grain; clip-art SVG.
- **Type**: the overused faces; a flat scale (no jump ≥ 1.25×); italic serif hero by reflex; extreme negative
  tracking; tracked caps everywhere.
- **Motion**: fade-up on scroll everywhere; bounce; pulsing dots; marquees; blinking cursors.
- **Copy**: buzzwords; "where X meets Y"; aphorisms in threes; em-dash chains; hedging; category words ("solutions",
  "experiences", "digital projects"); a tagline posing as a description.
- **Material**: imitation material (CSS bevels, embossing, faux metal or paper) is the single most reliable mark of
  machine-made design. Real photographs, real work, real data beat any effect.
- **Browser surfaces left at defaults**: blue selection, default focus ring, grey scrollbars on a themed page.

## The specificity tests (run every one before calling a view done)

1. **Cover the logo.** Could this view belong to another studio, agency or app? If yes, it isn't done. Name the one
   thing in each view only this product has.
2. **Swap test.** Swap the colours and fonts with a competitor's. If nothing now reads wrong, the system is generic.
3. **Skeleton test.** Strip the copy. Does the structure alone still say what the section is and why it matters? If it
   only works once the words return, the boldness is in the text size, not the design.
4. **Squint test.** Blur it. Primary, secondary and groups still read in order?
5. **Memory test.** After one viewport, what would someone describe an hour later? A mood is a fail.
6. **Neighbour test.** Could a neighbouring product use this moment unchanged? Then it isn't a signature.
7. **Removal test** (for effects). Would anyone notice it gone? If not, remove it.
