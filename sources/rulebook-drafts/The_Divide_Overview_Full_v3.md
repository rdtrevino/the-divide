# **THE DIVIDE: Rulebook**

*A game of divine ambition and hidden loyalties.*

**Created by:** Robert Treviño
**Players:** 4 to 6 | **Play Time:** 45 to 60 minutes
**Mechanisms:** Hidden Allegiance, Social Deduction, Variable Player Powers, Tableau Building, Negotiation, Tug-of-War

---

## **What This Game Is**

Before the first dawn, the unfinished world rests on the Divide, the narrow span between the Heights above and the Abyss below. You are a Celestial, a primordial power with a secret agenda for where the world should fall. Over six Days, you will push it toward the light, drag it toward the dark, or fight to hold it where it lies.

**To win, you need two things:**

1. When the game ends, the world must belong to your secret faction.
2. Among the players of that faction, you must hold the most Glory.

Faction victory makes you eligible. Glory makes you the winner. You must help your side win the world while out-earning the allies helping you do it.

---

## **The Five Things You Touch**

Everything in The Divide runs through five objects.

**The Balance.** An 11-space track in the center of the table with a single marker on it. The middle 3 spaces are the Divide. The 4 spaces on each end are the Heights (Light) and the Abyss (Dark). The marker's final position decides which faction controls the world.

**The Altar.** A shared pool of cards in the center of the table. Cards played here are public, and at the end of each Day their combined Light and Darkness decide which way the Balance moves. The Altar is emptied every night, and any card placed on it can be taken by another player before the Day ends.

**Your Choir.** Your personal row of 3 face-up cards, dealt full at setup. Its cards give you abilities, and each night the Choir is scored against the direction the world moved: lean with it and you gain Glory, lean against it and you lose it.

**Your Hand.** Your hidden Host cards, normally 3, refilled each night. This is the only hidden zone you control, and every card you play from it gives the table information.

**Glory.** Both score and currency. You earn it by playing cards and scoring your Choir, you spend it to build your Choir and to defect, and you may give it to other players to seal a deal. Glory never drops below 0. The most Glory within the winning faction takes the game.

### **Reading a Host Card**

The Host deck is the game's one deck of playable cards: 69 in total, split evenly into 23 Light, 23 Dark, and 23 Neutral or Variable. Every Host card shows:

* A **name** and an **ability**, with a cadence telling you when the ability triggers (on your turn, passively, once per game, and so on).
* An **Offering value**: Light +1 or +2, Dark +1 or +2, or Neutral. A card's Offering pushes the Balance while on the Altar and shapes your Choir's lean while in your Choir. Neutral cards push nothing and lean nowhere. A +2 Offering appears only on once-per-game cards: the deck holds exactly ten, five Light and five Dark, and a spent once-per-game card keeps its +2 value (see Used Card in the Glossary).

## **Components**

* **The Balance:** an 11-space tug-of-war track (3 Divide spaces flanked by 4 Heights and 4 Abyss spaces), with 1 tracking marker.
* **69 Host cards:** the main deck. 23 Light, 23 Dark, 23 Neutral or Variable.
* **9 Celestial cards:** unique player powers.
* **20 Event cards:** one drawn each Day, changing that Day's rules.
* **Fate tokens:** secret allegiance markers (Faithful, Fallen, Forsaken).
* **Turn cards:** permanent defection markers kept beside the Altar.
* **Glory markers** and **1 starting player marker.**

---

## **Setup**

1. **Draw secret Fates.** Pool Fate tokens by player count using the table below, mix them in the bag, and have each player secretly draw one. Leave the rest in the bag, unrevealed. Because leftover tokens stay hidden, no one knows the true headcount of any faction. There may not even be a Forsaken at the table.

| Player Count | Faithful | Fallen | Forsaken | Left in Bag |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| 4 Players | 2 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 5 Players | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 |
| 6 Players | 3 | 3 | 1 | 1 |

2. **Place the Balance** in the center with the marker on the middle space of the Divide.
3. **Set out the Turn cards** face up beside the Altar space: 1 card for 4 players, 2 cards for 5 or 6 players. The Altar itself starts empty.
4. **Deal Celestials.** Each player receives a Celestial card, face up. These asymmetric powers are public knowledge.
5. **Deal Host cards.** Each player takes a hand of 3 Host cards, kept hidden, then 3 more face up in a row in front of them. This is their starting Choir.
6. **Finish.** Shuffle the Event deck, set all Glory to 0, and give a random player the starting player marker.

## **How a Day Works**

The game lasts up to 6 rounds, called **Days**. Each Day has two phases. In the **Morning**, the Day's rules are revealed and every player takes one turn. In the **Evening**, the Altar is tallied, the Balance moves, and every Choir is scored.

### **Morning: The Unveiling**

Every Morning opens with a fixed three-step sequence, resolved before anyone takes a turn:

1. **The Event.** The first player draws the top Event card, reads it aloud, and places it face up. It changes the rules for the entire Day and applies to everyone equally. (If the Oracle is in play, they may first peek at the card and choose to play it or bury it.)
2. **Declarations.** Any required predictions are locked in now, before the Seed is revealed. The Whisper makes their public call, and if Casting Lots is the Event, every player commits a secret prediction.
3. **The Seed.** Flip the top card of the Host deck onto the Altar. This is the Seed, the round's starting offering.

### **Morning: Your Turn**

Starting with the first player and moving clockwise, each player takes exactly one turn: choose one of four actions, then activate your Choir.

**Offer.** Play 1 Host card from your hand onto the Altar and immediately gain 1 Glory. This is your income and your vote: the card's Offering now pulls the Tide, and the Glory is yours to keep even if the card is later taken. An Offer is provisional. Anyone acting after you can Gather it away before the Day ends.

**Gather.** Take 1 Host card from the Altar, or from the top of the Host deck, into your Choir. If your Choir is full, discard one of its cards to make room. This is how you improve for free, and how you play defense: stealing a Dark card off the Altar weakens the Dark Tide. But the card you take now shapes your Alignment, and a raid that flips the Tide can leave you holding the wrong card at scoring.

**Exalt.** Pay 1 Glory to play a Host card from your hand into your Choir, discarding to make room if it is full. This is the only way to move a card from your hidden hand into your Choir without it ever touching the public Altar. You pay for the privacy.

**Turn.** Once per game, defect to the opposing faction. See The Turn: Defection below.

**Then activate.** After your action, you may trigger the ability of each Host card in your Choir. Each card activates at most once per turn, and only in its printed cadence.

### **Evening**

When every player has taken a turn, resolve the Evening in this exact order:

1. **Evening abilities.** Trigger any Celestial or Host abilities that reference the Evening.

2. **Set the Tide.** Total all Light values on the Altar, total all Darkness values, and compare. The heavier side is the **Tide**, and the Balance marker moves toward it: 1 space for a lead of exactly 1, or 2 spaces for a lead of 2 or more (2 is the cap). A tie, or an empty Altar, means a Neutral Tide and no movement.

   > *Example: the Altar holds Light +2, Light +1, Dark +1, Dark +1, and a Neutral card. Light totals 3, Darkness totals 2. The Tide is Light by a margin of 1, so the marker moves 1 space toward the Heights.*

3. **The Reaping.** Every player scores their Choir. Find your **Alignment**: the net difference between the Light and Darkness values in your Choir (Neutral cards count nothing). If your Alignment matches the Tide's direction, gain Glory equal to your net value. If it opposes the Tide, lose that much (never below 0). A net-zero Choir gains and loses nothing. Gains and losses are capped at 3 Glory per Reaping, even after doubling effects.

   > *Example: your Choir holds Free Will (Light +2), Confession (Light +1), and Moloch (Dark +1). Your net Alignment is Light 2. If the Tide is Light, you gain 2 Glory. If the Tide is Dark, you lose 2. The Tide's margin does not matter here, only its direction.*

4. **Refresh the Altar.** Discard every card remaining on the Altar. Nothing carries over.

5. **Upkeep.** Everyone draws back up to their hand limit, normally 3 (or discards down to it), and the starting player marker passes left. If the Host deck is ever empty, shuffle the discard pile to form a new deck.

If the Balance marker has not ended the game (see Ending the Game), a new Day begins.

## **The Turn: Defection**

Once per game, during any Day except the last, a player may spend their action to **Turn**, defecting to the opposing faction. The Forsaken cannot Turn; they are bound to the center.

**To defect:** pay 1 Glory and place one of the face-up Turn cards from beside the Altar into your Choir, discarding to make room if it is full.

**The Turn card is a scar, not a tool.** It is not a Host card. It has no Offering, no Alignment, and no ability. It cannot be Targeted, discarded, replaced, or counted by any effect that references Host cards, and it occupies its Choir slot for the rest of the game.

**Defection is not instant.** You remain your original Fate until the end of the current Day; your new Fate takes hold at the next Unveiling. A Turn taken on the final Day fails to resolve in time, and you score as your original Fate.

**Defections are scarce.** The supply of Turn cards is the limit: 1 in 4-player games, 2 in games of 5 or 6.

## **Deals and Table Talk**

Open negotiation is permitted at all times, and the game expects you to use it.

* **Glory moves freely.** You may hand Glory to any player at any time, for any reason. Once given, it cannot be taken back.
* **Promises are not binding.** You may promise anything: future plays, alliances, safe passage. Betrayal is a legal, legitimate strategy. Trust accordingly.
* **Cards never change hands by agreement.** Host cards, Fate tokens, and Turn cards move only through explicit card actions and Celestial abilities. You cannot trade, gift, or sell them.

## **Ending the Game**

The game ends **immediately** if the Balance marker reaches the final space of the Heights or the Abyss. Otherwise it ends after the Evening of the 6th Day.

The marker's final position decides who controls the world:

* **The Heights:** the Faithful control the world.
* **The Abyss:** the Fallen control the world.
* **The Divide (center 3 spaces):** the Forsaken control the world.

**Then all Fates are revealed**, and each player gains 1 bonus Glory for every Host card in their hand and Choir that matches the winning faction's alignment.

**The winner** is the player with the most Glory among those whose Fate matches the controlling faction. Everyone else, however rich, has backed the wrong world.

**The Unclaimed Divide.** If the marker finishes in the Divide but the Forsaken token was never drawn at setup, no faction controls the world. The restriction lifts: the highest Glory total at the table wins outright, regardless of Fate. No endgame card bonus is awarded in this scenario.

## **The Heart of the Game**

Your secret Fate wants the Balance in one place. Your Glory wants your Choir aligned with wherever the Tide is actually going. These two goals regularly pull apart.

A Fallen player might spend a Day offering Light because their Choir leans Light and they need the points. A Faithful player might sandbag a round to avoid revealing their allegiance. The Forsaken profits most when the other factions cancel each other out. Every card you play gives the table information, and controlling how that information is read is where games are won.
