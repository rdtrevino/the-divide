# **THE DIVIDE: Rulebook**

*A game of divine ambition and hidden loyalties.*

**Created by:** Robert Treviño
**Players:** 4 to 6 | **Play Time:** 45 to 60 minutes
**Mechanisms:** Hidden Allegiance, Social Deduction, Variable Player Powers, Tableau Building, Negotiation, Tug-of-War

---

## **What This Game Is**

Before the first dawn, the unfinished world rests on the Divide, the narrow span between the Heights above and the Abyss below. You are a Celestial, a primordial power with a secret agenda for where the world should fall. Over six Days, you will push it toward the light, drag it toward the dark, or fight to hold it where it lies.

**To win, you need two things:**

1. When the game ends, the world must belong to your secret faction.
2. Among the players of that faction, you must hold the most Glory.

Faction victory makes you eligible. Glory makes you the winner. You must help your side win the world while out-earning the allies helping you do it.

---

## **The Five Things You Touch**

Everything in The Divide runs through five objects.

**The Balance.** An 11-space track in the center of the table with a single marker on it. The middle 3 spaces are the Divide. The 4 spaces on each end are the Heights (Light) and the Abyss (Dark). The marker's final position decides which faction controls the world.

**The Altar.** A shared pool of cards in the center of the table. Cards played here are public, and at the end of each Day their combined Light and Darkness decide which way the Balance moves. The Altar is emptied every night, and any card placed on it can be taken by another player before the Day ends.

**Your Choir.** Your personal row of 3 face-up cards, dealt full at setup. Its cards give you abilities, and each night the Choir is scored against the direction the world moved: lean with it and you gain Glory, lean against it and you lose it.

**Your Hand.** Your hidden Host cards, normally 3, refilled each night. This is the only hidden zone you control, and every card you play from it gives the table information.

**Glory.** Both score and currency. You earn it by playing cards and scoring your Choir, you spend it to build your Choir and to defect, and you may give it to other players to seal a deal. Glory never drops below 0. The most Glory within the winning faction takes the game.

### **Reading a Host Card**

The Host deck is the game's one deck of playable cards: 69 in total, split evenly into 23 Light, 23 Dark, and 23 Neutral or Variable. Every Host card shows:

* A **name** and an **ability**, with a cadence telling you when the ability triggers (on your turn, passively, once per game, and so on).
* An **Offering value**: Light +1 or +2, Dark +1 or +2, or Neutral. A card's Offering pushes the Balance while on the Altar and shapes your Choir's lean while in your Choir. Neutral cards push nothing and lean nowhere. A +2 Offering appears only on once-per-game cards: the deck holds exactly ten, five Light and five Dark, and a spent once-per-game card keeps its +2 value (see Used Card in the Glossary).

---

## **How a Day Works**

The game lasts up to 6 rounds, called **Days**. Each Morning, an Event card changes the rules for the Day, one card is revealed onto the Altar to start the pool, and every player takes exactly one turn, either feeding the Altar for Glory or reshaping their own Choir. Each Evening, the Altar is tallied: whichever side weighs more is the **Tide**, and the Balance marker moves that way. Every Choir is then scored against the Tide, the Altar is swept clean, hands refill, and a new Day begins.

---

## **The Heart of the Game**

Your secret Fate wants the Balance in one place. Your Glory wants your Choir aligned with wherever the Tide is actually going. These two goals regularly pull apart.

A Fallen player might spend a Day offering Light because their Choir leans Light and they need the points. A Faithful player might sandbag a round to avoid revealing their allegiance. The Forsaken profits most when the other factions cancel each other out. Every card you play gives the table information, and controlling how that information is read is where games are won.
